## 🔴 the two verdict rules this census enforces, and the traps on each side of them

**both fail in the same direction — toward a verdict that looks tidy and hides a gap.**

| the rule | the trap it closes |
|---|---|
| **a `critipath` cell may NOT rest at `itemized`** | by this catalog's grammar `itemized` on a critipath is a **declared gap**. `rule.require.experience-coverage`: *"an absent critipath … with no case that demonstrates it — is a blocker"*. ⚠️ **the repair is always the DEMO, never the verdict** — a cell that cites a walked step is already demoed, and to leave it `itemized` under-reports the coverage the walk bought |
| **`impossible` means barred by NATURE; a config bar is `forbidden`** | `impossible` = no attempt is even possible. a bar that a config fact creates is **nurture**, and nurture is `forbidden`. ⚠️ `cicd-pipeline × declare × ×4` is the worked case: *"no workflow applies `aws.auth`"* is a config fact, so those four are **forbidden** |

⇒ **a census is only as sound as its strictest grade**, and `impossible` is the one most easily
over-used, because a cell that *does not happen* and a cell that *cannot* read alike.

🔴 **and the distinction is never cosmetic — a `forbidden` cell owes a checkable invariant where an
`impossible` one owes none.** here the invariant is stated: **no github workflow applies
`provision/aws.auth`**, and `.github/workflows/provision.yml:21` is where a reviewer confirms it.
that is a line a future PR can violate; *"impossible"* would imply no one could.

⚠️ 🔴 **and the `itemized → demoed` rule is the one that RE-BREAKS, so check it per slice.** a new
axis value generates a whole slice of cells, and a slice authored under the old habit carries the old
defect — even where the rest of the file is already repaired. **`revoked` is the worked case**:
`grove-clone × obtain × revoked` (`case=7` `[t4]`) and `cicd-pipeline × {obtain, act} × revoked`

## 🔴 the `declare` stage ranges over three senses, and the third was absent

`declare` is not one act. a declarative provisioner declares a resource into three distinct end
states, and each is a separate experience:

| sense | what the provisioner does | the plan reads | demoed by |
|-------|--------------------------|----------------|-----------|
| **create** | the grant does not exist; declare it | `CREATE` ×3, every incumbent `KEEP` | `case=3` |
| **converge** | the grant exists and matches; re-apply | all `KEEP` | `case=6` |
| **revoke** | the grant exists and should not — 🔴 **TWO applies, in order**: strip the attachments, confirm, then upsert the trust statement | apply 1: `DESTROY` on the attachments · apply 2: `UPDATE` on the target alone | **`case=7`** |

⚠️ **the revoke sense is an UPSERT of the trust policy, never a DELETE of the role.**
`DeclaredAwsIamRoleDao.ts:35` declares `set.delete = null`, so *"delete the declaration"* is a
**silent non-revoke** — `case=7` `[t3]`/`[t3']`. and the upsert ends the **next** session, never a
**live** one (`[t4']`). ✅ **the role's ATTACHMENTS do delete** (three iam DAOs implement it), so the
power half of a revoke is expressible today — F8 candidate ⑤.

🔴 **and that is why the revoke sense is TWO applies rather than one, which this table said for four
rounds.** the runbook order is *stop the live session, then close re-entry*; declastruct applies in
**declared array order** and the array must read **role first**, so a single plan executes
**upsert → strip** — the exact reverse. ⇒ the two acts cannot share a plan, and a plan that fails
between them leaves the **worst** state: a warned holder with full permissions and an **unbounded**
window. the full argument is at `1.vision.yield.md`'s aha and `case=7`.

🔴 **so the revoke sense is not one experience — it is TWO, and only the PROVISIONER lives both.** a
`rogue-process` revoke (`case=5` `[t2']`) is a *rewrite* of an extant trust policy: one apply, no
attachments to strip, no order to respect.

| who revokes | applies | why |
|---|---|---|
| **provisioner** | **2** — strip, then upsert | must stop a live session AND close re-entry, and the two cannot share a plan |
| **rogue-process** | **1** — upsert | it only needs the door open; it never needs one shut |

⇒ **the attacker's act is cheaper than the defender's by exactly one apply**, which is the same
asymmetry the ≤1h tail carries (`[t4']`) read at the declaration layer instead of the runtime one.
**a walked product that grades `declare` by end-state alone cannot see this** — both are `UPDATE` on
a trust policy; they part on the number of applies the actor's *goal* requires.

`case=7` centres this sense; `case=4` `[t3]` and `case=5` `[t6]` each graze it from the far side of
another claim. **`1.vision.yield.md`'s stated aha is *"revocation by declaration"*, so the vision's
headline promise owes a walk of its own.**

🔴 **and the split ranges over EVERY actor that can `declare`, never the provisioner alone.** it is
declared as a property of the **axis**, so `rogue-process × declare` carries **two of the three**
senses too — `create` and `revoke`, never `converge`, which is defined by a plan verdict these actors
never read (`dimensions.md`) — and
its revoke sense (`case=5` `[t2']`, a rewrite of an extant trust policy, the incumbent's included)
is the sharper of the two. see `dimensions.md`.

### 🔴 the sub-cell count, reconciled — the split is ASKED of every declaring actor, and YIELDS a row only where the senses differ

the axis propagates the sense-split to **every** actor that can declare, and the count records **one**
sub-cell (`rogue-process × declare × both`). so where is `grove-clone`'s?

**it was asked, and it collapsed.** the two answers are not the same shape:

| cell | create sense | revoke sense | distinct experience? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rogue-process × declare × both` | **plant** a role an audit can find — `case=5` `[t2]` | **rewrite** an extant trust policy: no new arn, no new row, and persistence that survives `case=7` — `[t2']` | ✅ **yes** — different artifact, different audit signature, different survival |
| `grove-clone × declare × both` | same credential, benign intent | same credential, **same power** | ⛔ **no** — intent is not an experience at the aws layer |

⇒ **the propagation is of the QUESTION, never of the ROW.** a split asked of every actor and answered
per actor is a complete walk; a split *counted* for every actor would inflate the product with cells
that hold one experience each. **49 = 48 + 1**, and the collapse is a recorded verdict rather than an
omission a reader must infer.

⚠️ **the trap is the verb.** *"propagates"* holds two claims at once — *the axis property applies*
(true of both actors) and *a second row is generated* (true of one). ⇒ **an axis property and a
product row are different claims, and one verb holds both.**

### 🔴 the third collapse — `provisioner × declare × both`, converge folded into revoke

the `declare` stage carries three sub-senses (create / converge / revoke), and `dimensions.md` marks
converge reachable in **both** `both` and `target-only`. the walked product pins

⇒ **a recorded collapse, never an omission.** an all-`KEEP` re-plan is byte-identical whichever half

🔴 **and the collapse REASON differs from the two above, which is why it is recorded separately.** the
`grove-clone` collapse folds on *intent is invisible at the aws layer*; this one folds on *the axis
value is not an input to the mechanism*. ⇒ **two cells can merge because the actor cannot be told
apart, or because the axis cannot reach the code — and only the second is falsifiable by a file
read.**

🔴 **the rule the column obeys, stated so it is auditable rather than asserted: a demo's density
counts SEAMS CROSSED, never defects found.** a `[tn]` that deepens a counted boundary sharpens the
demo and moves no number; a `[tn]` that reaches a boundary no other step touches moves it. otherwise
the column drifts into a proxy for review effort, and stops to rank demos by what they cover.

two sit **after** it, in the state the revoke produces. ⇒ **a demo that stops at its own success
cannot count the seams its success creates** — which is the sharpest argument for the journey shape
this section holds.

### 🔴 the omission this table's own audit exposed

the `[t5']` row above had graded itself *"a limit on the attribution seam **already counted**"* —
while `case=5`'s boundary list held **no attribution seam at all.** the limit was priced against a
seam the list never carried, so `case=5` read **6** where it crosses **7**.

⇒ **the tell is exact and mechanical: a row that justifies "counts naught" by an ALREADY-COUNTED
seam names a position, and that position must be checked against the list.** here it named position 5,
which held *the absent permissions-boundary primitive* — a different boundary entirely. **no read of
either the list or the row alone could catch it; only a read of the row AGAINST the list.**

⚠️ **and the direction matters.** every other numeric defect this vision found was an **over**-count —
a claim that overstated coverage. this one **understated** it, which is why it survived: an
under-count reads as conservative, and conservative reads as safe. it is neither. it mis-ranks the
demos, and the density column exists precisely to rank them.

⇒ the shape the metric prescribes — **dense journeys for breadth, focused spotlights for each sharp
critipath's fail-safe** — is satisfied: `case=7` is the journey, `case=1`–`6` are its six spotlights.
