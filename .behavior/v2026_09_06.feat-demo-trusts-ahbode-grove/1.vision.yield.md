## the shape

`provision/aws.auth/account=demo/` gains one **new, dedicated** iam role,
**`ehmpathy-demo-for-grove`**. its trust policy holds exactly one statement, which names the **ahbode
camp grove role** in the ahbode camp account. the role carries `demoPermissionsPolicy` — the bundle
cicd already reads, unedited. `ehmpathy-demo-oidc` and the SSO permission set stay untouched.

🔴 **the bundle choice is RULED, never a best-guess.** it was the vision's crux fulcrum (**F1**) and
its one `[wisher]` crux question; the wisher settled it 2026-09-08: *"yeah this is fine; demo account
needs full access. no worries."* ⇒ **the shape above is the decided shape**, and what the verdict
accepts rather than resolves is stated at question 1 and in `case=5`.

⚠️ **the literal arn is deliberately absent from this paragraph.** it is recorded once, as two atoms,
in the groundwork table below, and derived from them at the declaration. **prose needs the referent,
never the value** — priced at **F6**, whose third axis records why a foreign org's account id cannot
be derived the way F3 derives ours.

🔴 **the shape is a FAMILY, never a one-off — `ehmpathy-demo-for-$x`, one per collaborator.** the
wish names one consumer; the wisher widened it to *"any third party collaborator account."* the answer
is ahbode's own form: they declare `ahbode-prep-for-grove` **and** `ahbode-prod-for-grove` — one role
per (target account × consumer), never one shared role that carries several parties' trust. ⇒ a second
collaborator is a second role, and every claim below holds per-collaborator unchanged (**F11**).

⚠️ **and each is a ROLE, never a user.** the reach is `sts:AssumeRole` against a trust policy, so no
long-lived access key exists to mint, ship, or rotate — which is what the *"no credential exists to
leak"* claim rests on. a demo **user** would trade that property away for a key file on a foreign
org's box.

## the outcome world

### before

a clone on an ahbode camp grove holds one credential it never asked for — the box's instance role,
ambient in IMDS. that badge reaches ahbode prep and ahbode prod. it has never heard of ehmpathy. so a
demo task stalls at the first aws call, and two workarounds are live today: hand-carry a keyrack
credential onto the box, or move the work off the box. this wish retires both.

### after

```sh
aws sts assume-role \
  --role-arn arn:aws:iam::<demo-account-id>:role/ehmpathy-demo-for-grove \
  --role-session-name grove
# -> a session credential, <=1h, principal assumed-role/ehmpathy-demo-for-grove/grove

export AWS_REGION=us-east-1                         # required — no ~/.aws/config on a grove
rhx git.repo.test --what integration --mode apply   # green, against demo
```

⚠️ **integration, and not acceptance.** acceptance drives the declastruct CLI, whose fresh process
sources credentials from **keyrack** rather than the ambient session — a vault a grove does not hold.
this wish delivers demo *integration* work from a grove; `case=1` `[t5]` walks the limit.

### the aha

**the clone received not one secret.** files in the repo that owns the account declare the whole
grant, so **an edit to the declarations ends the reach** — no key to hunt, no rotation to chase, no
leak to assume. that is what a credential handoff can never offer: **revocation by declaration.**

🔴 **and the grant is FOUR resources, never one — which is why the revoke is not one edit.**

| the resource | answers | a revoke must |
|---|---|---|
| the **role** | does the identity exist? | ⛔ cannot delete it (`set.delete = null`) |
| its **trust policy** | **who may assume it** | **upsert** — closes re-entry |
| its **inline attachment** | **what the holder may do** | **delete** — closes power |
| its **managed attachments** | same | **delete** — closes power |

⇒ **the trust policy answers WHO; the attachments answer WHAT.** a trust-policy rewrite alone leaves
every permission live on a role a held token still carries. that is why the orphan is
*"fully-permissioned"*, why F8 needs candidate ⑤, and why the revoke is **two applies**.

🔴 **and for the ROLE the verb is REWRITE, never delete.** `DeclaredAwsIamRoleDao` declares
`set.delete = null` (`:35`; same for policy, user, instance profile, oidc provider) and `src/**` holds
no `DeleteRoleCommand`. ⚠️ **three iam DAOs DO implement delete** — both role-policy attachments and
the user access key — so the limit is on **the role itself**, never on iam wholesale. ⇒ *"delete the
declaration and apply"* does **not** revoke; it leaves a fully-permissioned role live in demo, still
trusted by the grove and now unmanaged. walked at `case=7` `[t3]`/`[t3']`.

⚠️ **three qualifiers, since an incident is when they matter.**

**one — it needs a human.** the apply needs demo-admin creds, and that login is **interactive SSO** —
*"an agent CANNOT do this step"* (`howto.reapply-demo-oidc-role`).

🔴 **two — "the reach ends" is true of the NEXT session and false of a LIVE one.** sts issues a bearer
token with its own clock, so a trust-policy edit gates the next `AssumeRole` and cannot reach a token
already handed out. the revoke carries a **tail of up to 1h** (`case=7` `[t4']`), and under F1's
**ruled** bundle that tail carries `iam:` writes, so a hostile holder can spend it on re-entrenchment
(`case=5` `[t2']`). ⇒ the aws-native evidence: the iam console has a separate *"revoke sessions"*
action, which attaches an inline deny keyed on `aws:TokenIssueTime` — redundant if a trust-policy edit
ended live sessions.

🔴 **three — WHICH two acts depends on open question 11(d), and this vision states it two ways.**

| if question 11(d)… | the revoke is | why |
|---|---|---|
| **fails** | ① `TokenIssueTime` deny → ② trust upsert | only the deny reaches a live token |
| **holds** | ① strip attachments (F8 ⑤) → ② trust upsert | the strip reaches the live token, and it is **already expressible** — no `src/` change |

⇒ **the runbook cannot be written until 11(d) runs.** in both branches the **order is the same**: stop
the live session first, close re-entry second.

🔴 **and that order CANNOT be achieved in one declastruct apply.** declastruct applies in **declared
array order**, and the array must read **role first** (both attachments reference it — F5). so a single
plan that both upserts the trust policy and strips the attachments executes **upsert → strip** —
exactly the reverse of the runbook. ⚠️ and `applyChange` runs **per change** and can throw mid-plan, so
a failure between the two leaves the **worst** state: a warned holder, full permissions, and an
**unbounded** window rather than a ≤1h one. ⇒ **a revoke is TWO APPLIES** — strip, confirm, then upsert.

## mental model

### the words humans will use

> *"the grove can get into demo now."* / *"we opened a door from camp to demo."*

### the words the domain uses

the paired repo has already coined and evidenced the term: a **reach** is *"a curated, tiered,
cross-account capability: the PAIR of grants that lets one account's identity assume a named role in
another account"* (`term=reach`). its **target** is the ROLE, never the account. so this repo owns
**the target half of the camp→demo reach** — a vocabulary that already carries weight on the other
side of the same wire.

### the metaphor that fits

**a door with two locks, one key held in each of two buildings.** turn one and the door still does not
open — and it does not rattle either. it simply fails to open when someone tries it. that silence is
the whole of `case=2`: a green apply and a dead reach are perfectly compatible.

### where our word and their word disagree

⚠️ ahbode's `term=tier` names a ladder — `everyday-reader < everyday-power < emergency-admin` — and
reserves the admin rung for *"the acts that mint or rewrite identity itself (every `iam:` write)."*
**this repo has no tier ladder.** it has one bundle, `demoPermissionsPolicy`, and that bundle carries
`iam:CreateRole`, `iam:PutRolePolicy`, `iam:UpdateAssumeRolePolicy`, and `iam:CreateAccessKey` — every
one on `resource: '*'`.

| vs ahbode's everyday-power | demo's bundle |
|---------------------------|---------------|
| most services | **narrower** — `ReadOnlyAccess` + an explicit allowlist, not `PowerUserAccess` |
| `iam:` | **wider** — the whole write surface, which their ladder puts a rung above |

⇒ the wish's phrase *"everyday-power"* is a foreign vocabulary that describes a local artifact, and it
fits badly. so name the role for its **consumer**, never for a tier it does not sit on — hence
`ehmpathy-demo-for-grove` (F2).

## what is awkward

1. 🔴 **the wish's own sentence contradicts itself, and the contradiction is empirical.** *"enough for
   routine work"* and *"not enough to mint or rewrite identity"* cannot both hold, because the routine
   work mints identity. F1 chooses purpose and says so. it is the least comfortable call in this vision
   and the one most in need of a human verdict.

2. **we duplicate a factory that already exists, 500 metres away.** `getResourcesOfGroveReachTarget` in
   `ahbode/infrastructure` does precisely this job, and we cannot import it — foreign org, `provision/`
   directory, unpublished. wet-over-dry tolerates n=1 locally; across two repos this is n=3, and the
   natural home for the abstraction is `declastruct-aws`'s `src/`, which this wish scopes out.

   🔴 **and the cost is real, never merely a tidiness one.** what we cannot import is not the factory's
   *shape* — it is the **judgment inside it**. that file has already settled F1's fork, at two call
   sites, with a rationale that cites the same R1 constraint we cite. we re-derived that fork from
   scratch and reached a narrower answer. ⇒ **the duplication costs a re-litigated decision, and the
   second litigation was worse.**

3. **`demoPermissionsPolicy` is named for a trust level it no longer holds.** it began as *what an sso
   demo agent may do*; it became *what cicd may do*; it would become *what a foreign org's box may do*.
   a rename is out of scope and would be honest.

4. **the grant lives here; no test here can prove it.** this repo has zero `provision/**/*.test.ts`, and
   even a full suite could not prove the reach — the other half is in another org's account. ⇒ the
   honest resolution is that the claim itself must be scoped: we prove the **target half**, and we say
   plainly that the **reach** is unproven.

5. **the safest-looking option is the dishonest one.** a narrowed bundle would read as more careful in
   review and would break the suite on first use. that the cautious-looking choice is the wrong one
   deserves a flag, because a reviewer's instinct will pull toward it.
