# case=3 — the provisioner declares the grant, and the incumbents do not move

**cells** · `provisioner × declare × neither`
**feel** · happy  ·  **care** · **critipath**  ·  **boundary-density** · 2

> the wish's acceptance list is graded here. two of its six lines — *"`ehmpathy-demo-oidc` and the
> SSO permission set are unchanged — a diff proves it"* and *"applies clean"* — are exactly this
> path's `then`s.

---

## the narrative

**a human** authenticates as demo admin and plans the demo auth wish. the actor is not incidental:
`use.ehmpathy.demo --owner admin` is an **interactive** SSO login, and this repo's own runbook says
outright that *"an agent CANNOT do this step"* (`howto.reapply-demo-oidc-role`). so the whole grant —
its creation here, and its deletion in `case=7` — is gated on a person at a keyboard.

they hunt two facts, and the two are opposites.

first: **the new role appears.** `CREATE` on `ehmpathy-demo-for-grove`, `CREATE` on its policy
attachments. that is the wish delivered.

⚠️ **the human-only constraint has a consequence worth a word at the vision, not at execution:** a
driver who builds this grant **cannot verify their own work**. they can write the declaration; only a
human can apply it and read the plan. so "applies clean" is an acceptance criterion a driver must
hand off, and the verification stage should expect to say so rather than discover it.

second, and the one they actually squint at: **the incumbents do not move.** `ehmpathy-demo-oidc`
must read `KEEP`. its inline policy must read `KEEP`. the github oidc provider must read `KEEP`. a
single `UPDATE` on any of those means this wish touched cicd, and this wish must not touch cicd.

the reason that second read matters more is the shared bundle. `demoPermissionsPolicy` already feeds
two consumers — the SSO permission set in the root account and the OIDC role here — and the file
says so in its own hazard note (`provision/aws.auth/resources.common.ts:252-253`). a third consumer
that *reads* the bundle is free. a third consumer that *edits* it would bite in the shape the hazard
is named for: **local green, cicd red.**

⚠️ **and one correction to a phrase this vision inherited from the wish: there is no "third arm".**
the hazard's arms are **provisions**, never consumers — it names *"TWO SEPARATE provisions … two
different commands against two different accounts"*. the new role lands in the **same** provision as
`ehmpathy-demo-oidc`, so one `account=demo` apply covers both. what the third consumer really adds is
a **plan-read** obligation: the hazard's step 3 says *"confirm an `UPDATE` on the inline policy of the
role that target owns"* — singular — and `account=demo` now owns **two** roles.

so the plan diff is the proof. one `CREATE` cluster, and everything else `KEEP`.

then there is what the plan **cannot** tell them, worth a word at the moment of apply rather than at
the moment of failure: this apply proves the target half. it proves no part of whether a grove can
actually reach demo. that lives in another org's repo (`case=2`).

## what the two forms each caught

- the **narrative** caught *why* the `KEEP` rows matter more than the `CREATE` rows — the shared
  bundle and its named hazard. a `[tn]` alone would read `KEEP` as boring.
- the **`[tn]`** caught `[t1]`, which the narrative had folded away: the acceptance criterion is not
  merely "a role exists" but "its trust policy holds **exactly one** statement, and **no federated
  principal**". that is the mechanical form of the paired repo's *"a reach owns its target"* rule,
  and it needs a `then` of its own or it will go unchecked.

## the limit it holds

**one bundle, three readers, zero writers.** the wish adds a consumer of `demoPermissionsPolicy`
without an edit to it. so the `oidc-role-not-reapplied` hazard gains a documented third **consumer**
— a row in its role table — and **no new arm**, since the new role shares the `account=demo`
provision with the incumbent. an edit to that bundle would have been a different, much larger change.

⚠️ **the one new obligation is a plan-read, never an extra apply:** a `demoPermissionsPolicy` change
must now show **two** `UPDATE` rows in the single `account=demo` plan, where the hazard's step 3
expects one. an `apply` takes a whole plan, so it cannot update one role and skip the other — which
means one row where two are owed is a **mis-declared** role, never a partial apply. the old singular
check had no way to say that.
