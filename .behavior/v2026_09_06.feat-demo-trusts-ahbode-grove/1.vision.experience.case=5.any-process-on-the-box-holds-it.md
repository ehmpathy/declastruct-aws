# case=5 — any process on the box holds the reach

**cells** · `rogue-process × {obtain, act, declare} × both`  +  `grove-clone × declare × both`
**feel** · **sharp**  ·  **care** · **critipath**  ·  **boundary-density** · **7 — the densest spotlight**

> a **sharp critipath**, so it owes a fail-safe proof (`define.experience`). every containment claim
> in this vision meets here. this is the demo whose claims most deserve attack.
>
> 🔴 the decomposition *generated* this case. the wish never named it — it fell out of the
> `rogue-process × declare` cell of the product.

### ⚠️ why the feel is **sharp** when the actor glides

`define.experience._.axis=feel.path=happy-vs-sharp` defines feel as *"what the actor feels as they
walk the path"* — and by that letter this case reads **happy**: the rogue process gets exactly what
it came for, with no snag.

**that read is a trap, and it must be named rather than silently side-stepped.** feel sets the
**obligation** — a sharp path must prove it fails safe. so if an adversarial actor's feel is graded
from the adversary's own pov, every security hazard grades happy and **escapes the fail-safe proof
entirely**, which is the precise opposite of what the axis exists to force.

⇒ **for an adversarial actor, feel is graded from the pov of the party the behavior protects.** the
rogue process is the *agent* of the edge, never the one it cuts; the cut party is the demo account's
owner, and they are cut badly. hence **sharp**, and hence the fail-safe table below is owed.

⚠️ the same argument applies to `rogue-process`'s every itemized cell in the coverage contract. it
is stated once, here, and cited from there.

## the narrative

a clone works on the grove. beside it, a dependency's postinstall command runs, at an unprivileged
uid, on the same box.

that command can reach the demo account. it needs no key, no config file, no leaked secret. it curls
IMDS, gets the grove's badge, and calls `sts:AssumeRole` on `ehmpathy-demo-for-grove` — the same call
the clone makes in `case=1`, with the same result.

this is no hypothetical about a misconfigured box. it is a documented property of the platform:
ahbode's `term=camper` records that the grove box answers IMDS **for every uid**. so the credential
is scoped to the *box*, never to the clone that meant to use it. a grant to the grove is a grant to
everything on it.

what it can then do is decided entirely by the permission bound, which is fulcrum **F1** — 🔴 **ruled
2026-09-08: the wisher upheld the reuse of `demoPermissionsPolicy` unchanged.** *"demo account needs
full access."* so the honest answer is settled rather than provisional, and it is worth a plain
statement: **it can become admin of the demo account.** the bundle
carries `iam:CreateRole`, `iam:AttachRolePolicy`, `iam:UpdateAssumeRolePolicy`, and
`iam:CreateAccessKey`, each on `resource: '*'` (`provision/aws.auth/resources.common.ts:67-107`). one
`CreateRole` with a self-chosen trust document plus one `AttachRolePolicy` of `AdministratorAccess`
is the whole escalation, and it is two api calls.

and no deny can remove that without a break of the purpose. this repo's own suite mints iam roles —
`setIamRole.ts:71` issues `UpdateAssumeRolePolicyCommand`, `setIamRole.integration.test.ts` exercises
it against demo, and `provision/aws.infra/account=demo/resources.iam.ts:35-38` attaches a managed
policy to a role. so the actions that make escalation possible are the actions the grove came for.
they are the same actions.

⇒ so this case does **not** claim the edge is prevented. it claims the edge is **contained, bounded,
and legible**, names exactly which of those three is load-bearing, and names the one primitive that
would prevent it and does not yet exist here.

## how the sharp edge fails safe

⚠️ **the obligations and the volunteered claims sit APART, for the same reason `case=7`'s do.** the
bar `define.experience._.axis=feel.path=happy-vs-sharp` sets is five names — *fast · loud · teaches
the fix · the limit holds · no silent drop* — and *"prevented"* and *"isolated at runtime"* are **not
among them**. they are `rule.prefer.prevent-over-correct` (a nitpick-severity mechanic rule),
volunteered here because this case is a security exposure and the higher bar earns its own record.

🔴 **to grade yourself against a higher bar and report the shortfall is the opposite of a hidden
edge — but filed in the fail-safe table it READS as one.** so the obligations are answered first.

### the five obligations

| obligation | verdict | evidence |
|------------|---------|----------|
| ✅ **the bound is ours, and it is the trust policy** | ✅ **the load-bearing fail-safe** | `[t7]`. every renewal re-enters our trust policy, so the rogue's access is bounded by **a declared file in this repo**, re-evaluated ≤1h. ⚠️ `[t4']` retires the *clock* as a containment, which reads as *"no bound holds"* — the bound simply sits in a stronger place |
| ✅ **an exfiltrated token fails** | ✅ **available today, zero declared resources** | `[t8]`. an `aws:SourceIp` condition on the trust statement, bounded to the camp fleet's egress. a **condition on an allow**, so unlike F1's `Deny` it rests on **no** unverified premise (contrast question 7). named in F9 as a table row and never walked — which is exactly why it read as absent |
| **the account boundary holds** | ⚠️ **holds, and it is NOT the load-bearing claim it was recorded as** | `[t3]`. ahbode's target roles name the camp grove role as principal and no other (`resources.reach.ts:38`), so a compromised **demo principal** cannot pivot into ahbode. 🔴 **but the blast radius is a property of the PROCESS, never of the principal** — and `[t3']` shows the same process reaches ahbode prep and prod through the **ambient camp badge**, on a route that never touches demo. ⇒ *"the blast is one sandbox account"* was **false**; the actor's reach is **three** accounts. ✅ **the marginal read is what the wisher should price: +1 sandbox onto a process that already held two live ones** |
| 🔴 **the blast ORIGIN is one box** | ⛔ **it is the whole FLEET** | the principal is a **role**, so every camp grove box holds this reach — **and every box added later inherits it**, with no change or review in this repo. the origin set is governed in another org's repo and grows without our signal. see `dimensions.md`, axis A |
| **fast — the credential expires** | ✅ holds, and 🔴 **it is the ONLY terminator** | `[t4]`. 1h aws default, and this repo's role contract exposes no field to lengthen it. an exfiltrated session token rots on its own. ⚠️ **and no other act ends it** — `case=7` `[t4']` shows a trust-policy revoke does **not** reach a token already issued, so against a live hostile session the 1h clock is no backstop to the revoke; **it is the whole of it**, unless the `aws:TokenIssueTime` deny is also applied. 🔴 **and against an EXFILTRATED token the clock is the ONLY control at all** — a second one is expressible today and was never weighed: an `aws:SourceIp` condition on the trust statement, bounded to the camp fleet's egress, fails a token replayed off-box. see **F9** |
| **loud — the blast is attributable** | ⚠️ **holds at the ROLE grain, and NOT at the PROCESS grain** | `[t5]` — the dedicated role gives cloudtrail a principal distinct from cicd's, so *"a grove did this"* is answerable, and that is why `case=4`'s dedicated-role rule pays at incident time. `[t5']` — *"the clone or the rogue?"* is **not** answerable: same box, same role, same source ip, and a caller-chosen session name. see the grain trap below |

| **no silent drop** | ✅ holds | `[t6]` — the persistence residual is surfaced, never swallowed. `[t5']` — the attribution grain-limit is stated rather than implied |

⇒ **all five are answered, two of them by fail-safes this table had not carried.** the sharp critipath
is **handled**, at the bar the rule sets.

### 🔴 the two volunteered claims — a HIGHER bar, honestly failed

these are the residual, and they are why question 1 goes to the wisher. they are **not** fail-safe
obligations; they are `rule.prefer.prevent-over-correct`, stated because a security exposure deserves
the stronger question asked aloud:

| the stronger claim | verdict | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **the edge is prevented** | ⛔ **it is NOT** | `[t2]`. stated, never hidden. the close is a `permissionsBoundary` — one absent field, raised as open question 2 rather than absorbed |
| 🔴 **the incumbent is isolated at RUNTIME** | ⛔ **at the DECLARATION layer only** | `[t2']`. `case=4` proves our *wish* cannot touch `ehmpathy-demo-oidc`. it does not consider that the *credential our wish issues* holds `iam:UpdateAssumeRolePolicy` on `'*'` — so a rogue process can rewrite the oidc role's trust policy directly. the close is F1's spread+append `Deny`, which rests on open question 7. see below |

⚠️ **the honest summary a wisher should read: the edge FAILS SAFE and is NOT PREVENTED.** those are
two different statements and this case makes both. a reader who collapses them concludes either that
the exposure is handled (it is contained, not removed) or that nothing works (two controls do). ⇒
**the residual below is the subject of question 1, and it is a decision, never a defect.**

### 🔴 the second grain trap — `case=4`'s isolation is about DECLARATIONS, never CAPABILITY

`case=4` earns a real guarantee: a widen made to the grove's role cannot reach cicd, because the two
roles are separate declarations. that holds, and it is why the dedicated-role rule pays.

⚠️ **it is a claim about what OUR WISH can do. `[t2']` asks what the CREDENTIAL OUR WISH ISSUES can
do** — and under the ruled bundle the answer is: rewrite any trust policy in the demo account,
`ehmpathy-demo-oidc`'s included.

| the layer | isolated from cicd? |
|-----------|--------------------|
| our declaration — the wish, the plan, the apply | ✅ yes — `case=4` `[t0]`–`[t2]` |
| the shared `demoPermissionsPolicy` bundle | ⛔ no — `case=4` `[t2']` |
| 🔴 the **runtime capability** the grant confers | ⛔ **no, and this is the widest of the three** |

⇒ so the wish's *"dedicated role"* constraint isolates **four** layers, and the count of layers it
does **not** isolate is two, not one. both un-isolated layers trace to the same cause — the reused
bundle — which is why this sharpens F1 rather than open a new fulcrum.

### 🔴 the grain trap — an obligation that holds in `case=4` and does NOT transfer here

*"the blast is attributable"* was true when `case=4` proved it. it was then carried into this case,
which changed the question underneath it and left the words on top untouched:

| the case | the question an incident asks | cloudtrail |
|----------|-------------------------------|------------|
| `case=4` | *"was that a grove, or was that cicd?"* | ✅ **answers it** — two roles, two principals |
| 🔴 **the fleet grain** | *"WHICH grove box?"* | ✅ **answers it — see below** |
| `case=5` | *"was that the clone, or the rogue process?"* | ⛔ **cannot** — one box, one role, one source ip |

⚠️ **the session name is the only field that could part them, and the caller picks it.** an attacker
who wants to look routine passes `--role-session-name grove`. so the field that reads like an
identity is an *assertion by the party under suspicion* — the one place a log must never be trusted.

### ✅ the grain that IS answerable, and was understated — WHICH BOX

the grant is fleet-wide (`dimensions.md`, axis A), so *"which of the camp's grove boxes?"* is a real
incident question this case had not asked. **it has an answer.**

ahbode's own cloudtrail sample records the camp identity as
`<camp-grove-role-name>/<instance-id>` (`provision/aws.infra/account=camp/resources.grove-probe.ts:68`)
— the session name on the **camp** role is the **instance-id**. and the `AssumeRole` call into demo
is logged **in demo**, with the calling principal in its `userIdentity`. ⇒ **the box is recoverable
from the AssumeRole event**, then linked to the subsequent api calls by the access-key id.

| grain | answerable in demo's cloudtrail? | from |
|---|---|---|
| grove vs cicd | ✅ | the role name |
| 🔴 **which grove box** | ✅ **yes, indirectly** | the `AssumeRole` event's caller identity → correlate by access-key id |
| which process on that box | ⛔ no | needs the box's own log |

⇒ **a concrete cross-repo ask, worth one line to the paired tree:** the demo `AssumeRole` call's
`--role-session-name` is chosen by **ahbode**. if it passes the instance-id — as camp already does
for its own role — then every demo api call is attributable to a box **directly**, with no
correlation step. a one-line change on their side that removes a whole investigative hop on ours.

⚠️ **it does not close the process grain**, which stays the honest ⚠️ below. but it does mean the
fleet-wide scope this vision surfaced arrives with its own attribution already mostly in place —
**a cost and its mitigation found in the same pass.**

⇒ **the consequence for the sharp-edge contract:** *loud* is graded against *"can the responder tell
what happened?"* here, the responder learns a grove-shaped principal did it and must then reach
**outside aws** — to the box's own process/audit log — to learn which uid. that is a real
investigative step, not a dead end, so this is a **degrade, never a failure**. it is graded ⚠️ rather
than ⛔ for exactly that reason.

⚠️ **and the same permissions boundary named below would not fix this one.** a boundary prevents the
escalation; it does not make the actor legible. attribution at process grain is an ahbode-side
property (which uid may reach IMDS), so it is **flagged here rather than absorbed** — this repo
cannot close it from a trust policy.

### 🔴 the residual — named, not papered

**in-demo escalation and in-demo persistence are not prevented.** `[t6]` revokes future sessions; it
does not undo what `[t2]` created. an attacker who reached demo admin can leave behind a role trusted
by an arn of their own choice, and that role outlives the upsert of our trust statement.

the textbook containment for exactly this is an **iam permissions boundary** attached to the grove
target role — a ceiling that caps the role's *effective* permissions no matter what it later grants
itself, which would make `[t2]` produce a role the grove cannot use.

**this repo cannot express it.** `DeclaredAwsIamRole` declares `arn`, `name`, `path`, `description`,
`policies`, `tags` — and no `permissionsBoundary` (`src/domain.objects/DeclaredAwsIamRole.ts:18-55`);
a search of `src/**` finds the field in no file.

⇒ **this is a raised find, never an absorbed one.** the wish says: *"if you find you need a new
resource type, that is a real find — raise it rather than absorb it."* the find is narrower than a
resource type — one nullable field on an extant domain object, plus its `set` path. it is carried as
an open question in `1.vision.yield.md`, and it is the single highest-leverage change that would move
this case from *contained* to *prevented*.

### the deny that does NOT transfer

ahbode's grove target for **prep** denies `ssm:StartSession`, `ssm:SendCommand`, and
`ec2-instance-connect:SendSSHPublicKey` — the remote-root surface
(`provision/aws.auth/resources.reach.ts:76-96`). the same deny in **demo** would break the purpose —
this repo's own suites drive every one of those actions against demo boxes:

| denied there | driven here by |
|--------------|----------------|
| `ssm:StartSession` | `sdkSsmSession/setSession.ts:29`, via `ssmSshTunnel.integration.test.ts` + `ssmVpcTunnel/setSsmVpcTunnel.integration.test.ts` |
